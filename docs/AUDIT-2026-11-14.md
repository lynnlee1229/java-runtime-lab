# Depth audit · 2026-11-14

Read before changing code: README, VALIDATION, RuntimeLab.java, the Python runner/verifier, site exporter, saved run-1/2/3 JSON and the release record. The baseline already has real platform-thread saturation, three cancellation paths, seeded retry comparisons and real JFR. Those experiments and their published reference records remain intact.

Four gaps were found:

- Thread-pool bounds were demonstrated, but virtual-thread-per-task execution and downstream resource bounds were absent. A reader could mistake cheap threads for unlimited downstream capacity.
- Cancellation was measured on one platform worker, but did not verify returning downstream permits or cancellation while queued for one.
- The guide had references but no searchable course/resource registry or six prediction → execution → interpretation exercises.
- JFR covered actual custom transitions, sleep and park, but did not collect virtual-thread lifecycle events or explain the JDK 24 change to synchronized pinning.

The first gap is the implementation focus. BulkheadLab adds one controlled experiment with three admission policies and two cancellation controls. It also closes the related permit-accounting gap. The course layer teaches this experiment alongside the preserved baseline; it is not a replacement website or a new framework.

The existing Temurin 25.0.5+8 LTS installation was read directly and the official release was checked: jdk-25.0.5+8, published 2026-10-22. No version upgrade, preview flag or external runtime dependency was added.

MIT 6.172, Fall 2018, is selected for measurement, concurrency reasoning and work accounting. Its language is C; it does not teach Java virtual threads, Future cancellation or retries. Those contracts come from JEPs and Java SE 25 documentation. Vanderbilt's archive was also inspected, but its course landing pages mix historical course labels and undated offerings. We retain one specifically identified 2020 lecture PDF as supplementary reading and do not invent a semester or claim a current 2026 course.

The exercises are original small synthetic programs. They neither reproduce university assignments nor publish their solutions. Course materials remain at the original institution URLs. No enrollment, course credit, certificate or paid course access is included.
